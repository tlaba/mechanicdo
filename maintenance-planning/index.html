Section B–B · What’s included

B

The parts of a maintenance system that works

Take the pieces you need. Each one is built around your plant, your assets and your people.

W.1Process

Work order process

A request-to-close-out flow your team will actually use: who requests, who approves, how jobs get planned, and failure codes on close-out so your maintenance history means something.

W.2Strategy

PM program

Task lists, frequencies and procedures built from OEM manuals, failure history and asset criticality — so PMs catch real failure modes instead of filling a calendar.

W.3Plan

Job plans

Parts, tools, LOTO points and time estimates written into each job, so the person at the machine has what they need before the work starts.

W.4Schedule

Weekly scheduling

A weekly maintenance schedule coordinated with production, so planned work actually happens and the backlog stops growing.

W.5Data

CMMS setup and cleanup

An asset register, equipment hierarchy and criticality ranking structured so PMs, work orders and reports line up with the plant floor.

W.6Measure

Critical spares and KPIs

A critical spares list tied to asset criticality, plus PM compliance, backlog, MTBF and MTTR reported in a way your managers will use.

DDetail D · PEMAC

Built on PEMAC’s maintenance management program

PEMAC Asset Management Association of Canada runs the Maintenance Management Professional (MMP) program. The two certificates I hold cover exactly this work: maintenance work management — planning, scheduling and work coordination — and maintenance strategy development, which is how a maintenance program gets built to fit your assets.

Maintenance · definition

“All actions intended to retain an item in, or restore it to, a state in which it can perform a required function.”

Definition used by PEMAC Asset Management Association of Canada, from the Asset Management Council.

E

Questions about remote planning

Rather talk it through? Call (437) 881-7808

Q1Do you need to visit our plant?

No. Planning work is done from what you share — exports, documents, photos and calls. If your plant is in the Greater Toronto Area, I can also walk the floor in person.

Q2Do you work with plants in the United States?

Yes. Because the work is remote, US plants get the same service as Canadian ones. I work in Eastern Time — weekday evenings and weekends — and we’ll set a meeting schedule that suits your time zone.

Q3What does PEMAC have to do with it?

PEMAC Asset Management Association of Canada runs the Maintenance Management Professional (MMP) program. I hold its Maintenance Work Management and Maintenance Strategy Development Certificates of Achievement, I’ve completed MMP Module 7 on maintenance information management and CMMS, and I’m finishing the full MMP program.

Q4Can you do the hands-on work too?

In the Greater Toronto Area, yes: preventive maintenance, 24/7 breakdown response and shift coverage. See on-site services.
